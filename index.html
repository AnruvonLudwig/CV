About

I am studying towards a Bachelor of Computing at Belgium Campus. Software engineering and development best describe me, although I find that defining myself by a specific area somewhat limiting.

I am interested in creating tools that solve problems. The type of tool or problem can vary widely: from a mobile app for grocery shopping to a CLI tool that streamlines building and managing legacy C#/.NET projects on Linux. What interests me is the problem itself and whether I can develop a comprehensive mental model of it and visualise how the tool will be used.

I have found that I am particularly good at finding gaps and contradictions in concepts because I try to internalise them and build a comprehensive mental model. The downside is that this process becomes slower as concepts grow in complexity, so I tend to find shortcuts to speed things up, but doing so can reduce my ability to find gaps in logic.

Outside of computing, I do archery for the love of the art. I have a love-hate relationship with how it challenges my tendency to simply do, or to hyperfocus on a single problem and lose sight of everything else. A good shot requires my mind and body to be aligned as a whole while still being attentive to each individual step.

I use Linux because Linux gives me problems, problems that I am sometimes able to develop my own solutions for.

Projects

Most projects are currently private as I am not yet proud of the code, but the experience and value I have gained from them are still worth showing.

  • Lardy

    A shopping list and larder stock-tracking app built with Flutter/Dart and a Drift database. The single-list shopping feature is functional enough for everyday use.

    Personal · GitHub

    What I learnt
    • How not to structure a layered architecture.
    • A project can stall when a feature reaches the point of being “good enough” and further development requires reworking existing code.
    • Trying to take on too much at once while learning a new concept can slow a project down significantly.

  • C# Helper

    A CLI tool for configuring VS Code, scaffolding classes, and working with legacy .NET projects on Linux. Written in C#.

    Private · GitHub

    What I learnt
    • It is easy to fall into the trap of asking AI to develop something just to get it done.
    • How to write and structure unit tests.
    • How relying heavily on AI can make it easier to overlook design decisions that I should understand myself.

  • Bug Tracker

    A basic bug-tracking website built as a university group project using JavaScript, CSS, and HTML. Submitted in a messy state, but ultimately met the requirements of the marking rubric.

    Private · GitHub

    What I learnt
    • Avoiding the leadership role when it is needed can lead to compromises that ultimately affect the quality of the project.
    • Not fully taking on the leadership role made it harder to steer the project towards what I originally wanted to build.
    • Sometimes you have to accept a result that falls short of what you originally envisioned.

  • Festival Console

    A C# console application simulating a festival and its events, exploring object-oriented programming and basic multi-threading. Developed as a university group project.

    Private · GitHub

    What I learnt
    • How to structure a layered architecture more effectively than before.
    • Introducing a new concept into a group project without first establishing a solid foundation can lead to late nights learning the concept while building the project skeleton.
    • Clear communication and explanation are important when introducing unfamiliar concepts.
    • A loosely defined scope combined with capable developers can cause a project to grow rapidly in complexity.
